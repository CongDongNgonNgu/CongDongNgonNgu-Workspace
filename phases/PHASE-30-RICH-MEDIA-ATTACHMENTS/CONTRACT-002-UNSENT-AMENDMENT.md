## Problem and selected proof boundary

A committed consumed PUT claim can be cancelled before dispatch, and an earlier derivative failure can leave a later derivative claim unused. A negative HEAD cannot prove that an interrupted or delayed write will never commit. The positive-commit ledger currently cannot retire a genuinely unsent claim without retaining uncertainty forever. Never fabricate an ETag or positive provider settlement to resolve this case.

Add immutable put_abandoned_at distinct from put_settled_at. Every consumed claim has exactly one outcome: unresolved (uncertain, neither timestamp); committed (settled timestamp plus ETag, not uncertain); or certified unsent (abandoned timestamp, no settled timestamp/ETag, not uncertain, only DELETE_PENDING/DELETED). WRITTEN always requires a real committed result. Claim identity and physical key stay single-use; abandoned claims cannot receive a new producer, become WRITTEN or be recreated.

Two internal authorities are both mandatory: the exact owner terminalized the attachment and awaited all actual local continuations/file/native producers for this claim; the adapter atomically retired the SAME ORIGINAL issued supervision entry before its actual SDK send ever STARTED. A locator match alone is insufficient. Each consumed claim gains a write-once put_supervision_id binding the unique original adapter entry identity before any enabled-runtime physical dispatch. Registering a replacement identity for that claim fails, including after releasing the old in-memory handle. Both the sealed local-closure capability and unsent certificate bind the exact consumed claim plus this persisted original entry identity.

Enabled dispatch must receive that captured handle through a mandatory supervised PUT method, reject released/retired/different-locator handles and never fall back to the legacy unsupervised PUT API. The adapter atomically chooses STARTED or RETIRED for the exact captured entry. retireUnsent returns an opaque entry/locator-bound certificate only for ISSUED; STARTED/SETTLED reject even when the SDK rejected. A newly issued handle for the same locator cannot hide prior dispatch, because its new entry identity cannot replace put_supervision_id and its certificate cannot satisfy the durable original binding. Reconstructed claims after restart cannot manufacture the old local-closure capability. Ordinary timeout, abort request, expired lease, public rejected promise, negative HEAD, process boot or a missing capability is not proof. Legacy unsupervised adapter calls remain only an internal compatibility boundary outside enabled runtime and cannot produce an accepted certificate.

The infrastructure ledger verifies the sealed certificate plus exact consumed claim and actual local-closure capability under existing system account/domain -> attachment -> object locks. Only terminal REJECTED/DELETED entities qualify. Persist abandonment, keep exact ownership and quota debt, and queue cleanup. No provider I/O occurs under SQL locks. Backend authorization remains authoritative; this infrastructure proof grants no user reads or parent mutation.

Cleanup may use absence-only validation for a certified-unsent claim: independent exact HEAD and bounded exact-key listing must both show absence. An unexpected positive object lacks a persisted ETag and therefore fails closed with an ownership/integrity alert, never blind deletion. Physical completion accepts the explicit abandonment outcome as resolved producer uncertainty, while still requiring all objects/jobs/producers settled and the existing reservation/retention guards. Upload/global processing slots require separate actual local producer closure; absence alone cannot release them. Seven-day history remains mandatory.
